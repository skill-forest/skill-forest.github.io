THE IDEA

Textual-gradient Skill optimization is subject to stochastic noise, making the optimization process high-variance and unstable. Skill-Forest grows independent Skill optimization trees on different training–validation views, combining a shared expansion budget, gain-prioritized expansion, validation-guided pruning, and early rejection for efficient forest construction. It then aggregates the tree-local winners through hierarchical merge or direct selection to produce one deployable Skill, without updating model parameters or relying on a stronger optimizer model.

ABSTRACT

From single-path Skill optimization
to random-forest-style ensemble optimization.

Agent Skills adapt frozen large language model agents by encoding reusable procedural knowledge outside model parameters. Under limited data, noisy textual updates and repeated selection on small validation sets can make single-path optimization sensitive to early decisions and evaluation noise.

We introduce Skill-Forest, an optimizer-level ensemble inspired by the randomize-and-aggregate principle of Random Forests. Each tree uses an independently sampled training–validation split and independently searches for its tree-local best Skill. Gain-prioritized expansion under a shared budget, validation-guided pruning, and early rejection enable efficient forest construction. The resulting tree-local best Skills are then aggregated into one deployable Skill through direct selection (Select) or hierarchical merge (Merge). This design diversifies both optimization paths and validation views to reduce the high variance associated with textual-gradient optimization.

We evaluate Skill-Forest across five benchmarks spanning direct-chat tasks, tool use, and persistent environment interaction. Seven-tree Select and Merge improve the mean held-out Hard score over No-skill by 12.82 and 12.87 percentage points, respectively. They exceed SkillOpt by 2.59 and 2.63 percentage points, respectively. Seven-tree Skill-Forest reduces training and validation rollouts by 71.68% relative to complete depth-three ternary-tree construction, supporting efficient multi-path Skill optimization.

CONTRIBUTIONS

Diversity, efficiency, and ensembling.

01 / DIVERSITY

An ensemble of Skill optimizers

Independent trees, persistent branching, and tree-specific data splits diversify both textual update paths and validation views in a capability-matched setting.

02 / EFFICIENCY

Budgeted forest construction

A shared expansion budget, gain-prioritized growth, validation-guided pruning, early rejection, and cached Root scores focus computation on promising branches.

03 / ENSEMBLING

Produce the final Skill through Select / Merge

Direct selection (Select) or score-guided hierarchical merging (Merge) aggregates tree-local winners into the final deployable Skill. Merge combines adjacent Skills at each level, retaining compatible rules and using comparable scores on the complete training split to guide conflict resolution, until one Skill remains.

METHOD

From independent trees to a single Skill.

All trees begin with the same initial Skill and explore their own local optima using execution feedback from independent training–validation views.

1

Randomize the data views

Independently partition the released training split into tree-local training and validation subsets. Training trajectories generate patches; validation evaluates them.

2

Grow and prune

Sample multiple patches per expansion. Retain children with positive parent-relative validation gain, and prioritize accepted nodes within a forest-wide budget.

3

Aggregate the winners

Keep one positive-gain winner per eligible tree. Evaluate winners on the complete training split, then select one or hierarchically merge them.

Exact early rejection

During incremental validation, reject a candidate as soon as even perfect scores on all remaining tasks cannot exceed its parent. This preserves the candidate set obtained by full validation.

Shared Root validation

Cache the initial Skill’s score on each distinct validation task. Every tree computes its own Root score from the cache, avoiding repeated evaluation on overlapping tasks.

Gains vary by task

Seven-tree Merge achieves the highest overall score among deployable methods in the draft. Neither Merge nor Select is uniformly better, and Skill-Forest does not outperform every baseline on every benchmark.

Efficiency has a defined scope

The 71.68% reduction uses summed training and validation counts across the five benchmarks, relative to complete depth-three ternary-tree construction. It excludes final selection, aggregation, and held-out testing.

ANALYSIS

The performance–cost trade-off.

Repeated optimization runs on LiveMath and ALFWorld examine the effects of tree count, branching breadth, and parallel trajectories.
